### II. LEGAL FRAMEWORK AND STANDARD OF PROOF

Under INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(iii) and 8 CFR § 204.2(c)(1), a self-petitioning spouse of a U.S. citizen must establish each of the following eligibility requirements (`USCIS_Policy_Manual_Volume_3_Part_D_VAWA.pdf`, 3 USCIS-PM D.2(A), PDF p. 6 / PM p. 947):
1. **Proper Filing:** The Form I-360 is properly completed, signed, and filed in accordance with form instructions (3 USCIS-PM D.4(A), PDF p. 47 / PM p. 1008).
2. **Qualifying Relationship:** The self-petitioner has a qualifying marital relationship to an abusive U.S. citizen spouse (3 USCIS-PM D.2(B)(1)–(2), PDF pp. 7–10 / PM pp. 948–951).
3. **Eligibility for Immigrant Classification:** The self-petitioner is eligible for immigrant classification as an immediate relative under INA § 201(b)(2)(A)(i) and is not barred under INA §§ 204(c), 204(g), or 204(a)(2) (3 USCIS-PM D.2(D), PDF pp. 21–22 / PM pp. 962–963).
4. **Good-Faith Marriage:** The self-petitioner entered into the qualifying marriage in good faith and not for the purpose of evading immigration laws (3 USCIS-PM D.2(C), PDF pp. 20–21 / PM pp. 961–962).
5. **Battery or Extreme Cruelty:** During the qualifying marriage, the self-petitioner was battered or subjected to extreme cruelty perpetrated by the U.S. citizen spouse (3 USCIS-PM D.2(E), PDF pp. 22–26 / PM pp. 963–967).
6. **Joint Residence:** The self-petitioner resides or has resided with the abusive U.S. citizen spouse during the qualifying relationship (3 USCIS-PM D.2(F), PDF pp. 26–27 / PM pp. 967–968).
7. **Good Moral Character:** The self-petitioner is a person of good moral character (3 USCIS-PM D.2(G), PDF pp. 27–32 / PM pp. 968–973).

* **Standard of Proof:** The burden of proof rests on the self-petitioner to establish eligibility by a **preponderance of the evidence**, meaning that the claimed facts are "more likely than not" or "probably" true (`USCIS_Policy_Manual_Volume_3_Part_D_VAWA.pdf`, 3 USCIS-PM D.2(A), PDF p. 7 / PM p. 948; 3 USCIS-PM D.5(B)(1), PDF p. 52 / PM p. 1013; citing *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010)).
* **Any Credible Evidence Provision:** Pursuant to INA § 204(a)(1)(J) and 8 CFR § 204.2(c)(2)(i), USCIS must consider "any credible evidence" relevant to the self-petition, while retaining sole discretion to determine what evidence is credible and the weight to afford that evidence (`USCIS_Policy_Manual_Volume_3_Part_D_VAWA.pdf`, 3 USCIS-PM D.2(A), PDF p. 7 / PM p. 948; 3 USCIS-PM D.5(B)(2), PDF pp. 53–55 / PM pp. 1014–1016). A self-petition may not be denied for failure to submit a particular type or piece of evidence (3 USCIS-PM D.5(B)(2), PDF pp. 53, 55 / PM pp. 1014, 1016). Officers evaluate each piece of evidence individually and in the totality of the circumstances for relevance, probative value, internal and external consistency, detail, and corroboration.

#### 6. Subjected to Battery or Extreme Cruelty (3 USCIS-PM D.2(E)) — **MET**
Under INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(I)(bb), 8 CFR § 204.2(c)(1)(vi), and 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E)(1) (PDF pp. 22–26 / PM pp. 963–967), the self-petitioner must establish that her U.S. citizen spouse battered her or subjected her to extreme cruelty during the qualifying marriage. Based on a holistic evaluation of the record, the self-petitioner has established **both** battery and extreme cruelty by a preponderance of the evidence.

##### A. Finding of Battery
The USCIS Policy Manual defines battery as follows:
> "Battery generally includes any offensive touching or use of force on a person without the person’s consent. Some examples include, but are not limited to, punching, slapping, **spitting**, biting, kicking, choking, kidnapping, rape, molestation, forced prostitution, sexual abuse, and sexual exploitation." (`USCIS_Policy_Manual_Volume_3_Part_D_VAWA.pdf`, 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E)(1), PDF p. 24 / PM p. 965).

* **August 2024 Spitting Incident:** In her affidavit, the petitioner provides a detailed account that in August 2024—during the qualifying marriage and while residing together—she noticed that $600 she had set aside for rent had been withdrawn from their joint bank account (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 14). When she told Mr. Lattimore that they could not afford for him to lend the rent money to a friend, Mr. Lattimore got in her face, cursed at her, and **spat on her**, hitting her face and neck, causing her to flee into the bathroom and lock the door (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 14).
* **Contemporaneous and Third-Party Corroboration:**
  * Ms. Barrett attests that in August 2024, the petitioner called her late at night "crying so hard I could barely understand her," and contemporaneously reported that she and Mr. Lattimore had argued over his taking $600 of rent money from their account, that he had cursed at her and spat in her face, and that she had locked herself in the bathroom (`Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 9).
  * Mr. Reid attests that in 2025, the petitioner cried while recounting to him that Mr. Lattimore had spat on her during an argument (`Affidavit_Desmond_Reid.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 11).
  * Licensed Clinical Social Worker Gregory T. Hale documents that the petitioner consistently reported the August 2024 spitting incident during clinical therapy sessions and continues to suffer intrusive posttraumatic memories of the event (`Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 2, §§ 4–5; p. 3, § 9).
* Because the USCIS Policy Manual explicitly identifies non-consensual **spitting** as an offensive touching constituting **battery** (3 USCIS-PM D.2(E)(1), PDF p. 24 / PM p. 965), and because this incident is credibly detailed by the petitioner and corroborated by three independent sources (including a contemporaneous outcry witness), the record establishes that the petitioner was subjected to battery during the qualifying marriage.

##### B. Finding of Extreme Cruelty
Even independent of the physical battery, the totality of the evidence establishes that Mr. Lattimore subjected the petitioner to **extreme cruelty** between March 2024 and May 2025. Under 8 CFR § 204.2(c)(1)(vi) and 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E)(1) (PDF pp. 23–25 / PM pp. 964–966):
* Extreme cruelty includes "any act or threatened act of violence... which results or threatens to result in physical or mental injury. Psychological or sexual abuse or exploitation... shall be considered acts of violence. Other abusive actions may also be acts of violence under certain circumstances, including acts that, in and of themselves, may not initially appear violent but that are a part of an overall pattern of violence."
* While "[h]urtful conduct alone, or adverse interactions of limited severity, are not sufficient to establish 'extreme' cruelty," conduct rises to the level of extreme cruelty when "[t]he actions... reflect an intention to gain or maintain power and control over the self-petitioner" and endanger the physical or mental health of the spouse (3 USCIS-PM D.2(E)(1), PDF p. 23 / PM p. 964).

Here, the record demonstrates a pervasive pattern of coercive control, psychological degradation, sexual coercion, economic exploitation, and immigration-related intimidation that went far beyond ordinary marital discord:
1. **Weaponization of Immigration Status and Sabotage of Adjustment Proceedings:**
   * As recognized in 3 USCIS-PM D.1(B) (PDF p. 2 / PM p. 943), abusive U.S. citizens often "use their control over this [family-based petitioning] process as a tool to further abuse the alien, threatening to withhold or withdraw the petition in order to control, coerce, and intimidate their family members."
   * Whenever the petitioner complained about Mr. Lattimore's mistreatment, he threatened her immigration status, telling her that she needed him for her green card and that he could have her "sent back to Jamaica whenever he wanted" (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 16; corroborated by `Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 11, and `Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 2, § 4).
   * He then acted on these threats by refusing to appear for their scheduled USCIS adjustment of status interview in April 2025 and refusing again to attend the rescheduled interview later that month, asking her, *"Why should I help you get your papers?"* (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 16; corroborated by `Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 11; `Affidavit_Desmond_Reid.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 11; and `Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 2, § 4).
2. **Physical Intimidation, Systematic Verbal Degradation, and Public Humiliation:**
   * After losing his warehouse job in February 2024 and hiding it for three weeks, Mr. Lattimore reacted to the petitioner's inquiry in early March 2024 by jumping up, pointing his finger directly in her face, and shouting so loudly and closely that she backed into the kitchen counter in fear (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 2, ¶¶ 8–9; corroborated by `Affidavit_Desmond_Reid.pdf`, p. 1, ¶ 9; `Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, p. 1, ¶ 6; `Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 2, § 4).
   * Thereafter, he subjected her to ongoing verbal degradation: yelling at her if dinner was not ready when he wanted, pushing his plate away and calling her cooking "slop" and "garbage," telling her she could not do anything right, calling her "stupid," "useless," "worthless," and vulgar epithets, and telling her that no other man would ever want her (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 10).
   * Crucially, this verbal abuse is corroborated by a direct earwitness: Ms. Barrett attests that during numerous Sunday phone calls in the spring and summer of 2024, she personally overheard Mr. Lattimore in the background shouting at the petitioner, calling her "stupid" and "useless," using vulgar profanities, calling her food "slop," and telling her that no other man would ever want her (`Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, pp. 1–2, ¶¶ 6–7; `Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 13). On one Sunday in summer 2024, Mr. Lattimore yelled so loudly that Ms. Barrett heard every word from another room before the petitioner abruptly hung up and called back an hour later in tears (`Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 8).
   * In June 2024, after staying out all night without answering his phone, Mr. Lattimore publicly humiliated the petitioner at his friend Marlon's house in Irvington, screaming at her in front of his laughing friends to "stop embarrassing him and to stop tracking him down like a child," leaving her crying and humiliated (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 11; `Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 2, § 4).
3. **Sexual Coercion:**
   * During this period, Mr. Lattimore demanded sexual relations from the petitioner even when she told him she was exhausted from working long shifts or did not want to participate; when she declined, he responded with anger, sulking, and accusations of infidelity until she relented to avoid conflict (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 12; `Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 2, § 4). Because he was staying out all night and hiding his phone, she felt degraded and feared contracting a sexually transmitted disease, prompting her to visit her physician for testing (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 12).
4. **Economic Control and Financial Exploitation:**
   * Following his termination in February 2024, Mr. Lattimore refused to seek employment and slept until noon while expecting the petitioner to work long shifts as a home health aide, pay all rent and household bills, and perform all cooking, cleaning, and laundry (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 10; `Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 10; `Affidavit_Desmond_Reid.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 11).
   * Although he contributed no income, he exercised control over their joint bank account containing her wages, repeatedly withdrawing cash and lending money to friends (including the $600 rent withdrawal in August 2024) while she struggled to pay rent and utilities (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶¶ 14–15). In early 2025, their joint federal tax refund was seized to satisfy an undisclosed prior debt of Mr. Lattimore's; when questioned, he told her to mind her business and declared that it was *"his name, his money"* (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 15; `Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 2, § 4). On May 17, 2025, he moved out while she was at work, taking the television and furniture and draining most of the remaining funds from their joint account (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 17; `Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 12).
5. **Clinically Documented Mental Injury (Psychosocial Assessment Report):**
   * Under 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E)(2) (PDF p. 25 / PM p. 966), psychological evaluations prepared by a qualified mental health professional who treated or thoroughly evaluated the self-petitioner using well-established assessment tools are recognized evidence of battery or extreme cruelty.
   * The Psychosocial Assessment Report (`Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, pp. 1–4) was prepared by Gregory T. Hale, LCSW (New Jersey License No. `[LICENSE NO.]`), a trauma-focused therapist at Hale Counseling Services, LLC (`Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, pp. 1, 4). Unlike a single-session forensic evaluation, Mr. Hale treated the petitioner across **eight outpatient therapy sessions** (~9 hours of direct clinical contact between June 12, 2025, and September 18, 2025) following a clinical referral from her primary care physician (`Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 1, §§ 1–2; `Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, pp. 3–4, ¶ 19).
   * Mr. Hale conducted Mental Status Examinations at each session and administered three well-established, standardized psychometric instruments at intake (06/12/2025) and re-test (09/18/2025):
     * **PHQ-9 (Depression):** Score of 19 at intake (moderately severe) and 14 at re-test (moderate);
     * **GAD-7 (Anxiety):** Score of 15 at intake (severe) and 11 at re-test (moderate); and
     * **PCL-5 (PTSD Checklist for DSM-5):** Score of 49 at intake and 41 at re-test, both substantially above the clinical PTSD threshold of 31–33 (`Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 1, § 2; p. 3, §§ 6–7).
   * Based on these longitudinal assessments, Mr. Hale diagnosed the petitioner under DSM-5-TR with **Major Depressive Disorder, single episode, moderate (F32.1)** and **Posttraumatic Stress Disorder (F43.10)**, along with **Z69.11 (Encounter for mental health services for victim of spouse or partner psychological abuse)** (`Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 3, § 8). He documented her severe clinical symptoms—including persistent tearfulness, initial and middle insomnia (4–5 hours of sleep per night), an approximate 15-pound weight loss, social and religious withdrawal, intrusive memories of the August 2024 spitting incident and shouting, exaggerated startle response to raised voices, and physiological hypervigilance upon hearing keys at the apartment door—and concluded that there was no indication of exaggeration or symptom fabrication and that Mr. Lattimore's pattern of psychological abuse and coercive control is the primary cause of her Major Depressive Disorder and PTSD (`Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, pp. 2–3, §§ 5, 9; corroborated by `Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 18; `Affidavit_Desmond_Reid.pdf`, pp. 1–2, ¶ 10; and `Affidavit_Simone_Barrett.pdf`, p. 2, ¶ 10).
   * In addition, the petitioner reasonably and credibly explained why she did not contact law enforcement during the marriage—citing shame, a belief that police would not intervene in non-visible abuse, and fear that calling the police would escalate the abuse at home and jeopardize her immigration case (`Affidavit_Marcia_Whitfield.pdf`, p. 3, ¶ 19; `Psychosocial_Report_Hale.pdf`, p. 2, § 4). Under the "any credible evidence" standard (3 USCIS-PM D.5(B)(2), PDF p. 53 / PM p. 1014), police reports or protection orders are not required.

#### 7. Good Moral Character (3 USCIS-PM D.2(G)) — **MET**
Under INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(bb), 8 CFR § 204.2(c)(1)(vii), and 3 USCIS-PM D.2(G)(1)–(3) (PDF pp. 27–31 / PM pp. 968–972), a self-petitioner must demonstrate that she is a person of good moral character, generally focusing on the 3-year period immediately preceding filing, evaluated under INA § 101(f) and the standards of the average citizen in the community. Primary evidence consists of the self-petitioner's affidavit accompanied by local police clearances or state criminal background checks (`USCIS_Policy_Manual_Volume_3_Part_D_VAWA.pdf`, 3 USCIS-PM D.2(G)(3), PDF pp. 30–31 / PM pp. 971–972; 8 CFR § 204.2(c)(2)(v)).
