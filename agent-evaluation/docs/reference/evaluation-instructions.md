## Stage 5: Overall preference and rationale

Compare the two results side by side and say which you would rather receive from a colleague. Use an *Unsure or negligibly better* option only when the two are almost equal. Then write your rationale:

### How to write your rationale

Write an expert-level comparison (at least 150 words) that explains your preference. Cover each result's key strengths and weaknesses on its own, what separated the two, and why the preferred result matters more in a professional context. For every issue, give the what (specific file and location) and the why. Do not critique the agent's process, speed or reasoning style; judge the deliverables.

### Bad examples: not acceptable

These are too vague, too short, or focus on the wrong things. Don't write feedback like this.

#### Bad Example 1

> “Antigravity gets the overall answer more concrete and better”

**Why it's bad:** Insufficient detail. What is “better”? What were the issues with Gemini Native's response?

#### Bad Example 2

> “Antigravity was slightly better in style and presentation.”

**Why it's bad:** What about the style or presentation was better? No specifics.

#### Bad Example 3

> “Marginal difference, both fundamentally incorrect”

**Why it's bad:** If both responses are incorrect, the worker should explain in detail *how* each one is incorrect.

### Mediocre examples: could be improved

These are heading in the right direction but stop short. Often they identify one real difference but skip the per-response analysis or miss other meaningful differences.

#### Mediocre Example 1

> “Similar outputs, but Gemini Native fundamentally misapplied the TV calculation, basing it off regular final year cash flows rather than the specific TV cash flows required to calculate the TV. Hence, the key difference was in the TV.”

**What's missing:** Decent comparative analysis (assuming there were no other substantive differences) but does not analyze each model’s output on its own.

#### Mediocre Example 2

> “Outputted values of Gemini Native match expected values exactly. Model logic is MUCH easier to follow than Antigravity.”

**What's missing:** Focuses on the model logic rather than the work product. Also doesn't describe the issues with Antigravity. What did it get wrong, and why?

#### Mediocre Example 3

> “Antigravity is the better response because it lays out all the criteria for this petition. It also cites to Tullier v. Tullier, which is a great case to cite as the facts in Tullier are very close to the facts of this client's case, and is very persuasive towards this petition being granted.”

**What's missing:** Decent comparative analysis, but doesn't explicitly describe the issues with Gemini Native, and doesn't describe any issues with Antigravity (assuming they exist).

### Good examples: what we want

These cover both individual and comparative analysis, name specific issues with the “what” and the “why,” and read like a domain-expert evaluation. Note that good feedback doesn't have to be long. Substance and depth of insight matter more than verbosity. “Golden” and “GAF” in these examples mean the reference answer; in this project, that is the reference solution description (the minimum standard).

#### Good Example 1: terse but substantive

> “Both models share common errors in SBC handling, FCF calculation, and understating capex by applying capex percentages to organic revenue only. Antigravity additionally funds bolt-on overflow into mezz during the projection period. Despite these shared issues, Antigravity's returns in both LBO scenarios land within ~25bps of the golden response, suggesting offsetting errors that keep the output directionally reliable. Gemini Native's inflated Adj. EBITDA calculation severely overstates returns, producing a ~200bps spread versus the golden response, which is large enough to mislead an IC discussion on whether the deal clears the hurdle. Antigravity is slightly preferred because its outputs remain usable for decision-making despite its errors, whereas Gemini Native's returns inflation would require material rework before presenting to a partner.”

**Why it works:** Calls out specific shared errors, identifies what each run uniquely did, and connects the differences to a real-world consequence (decision usability vs. needing rework).

#### Good Example 2: structured comparative summary

> “Where Gemini Native is better: Gemini Native fixed both WACC scaffolding bugs (Hamada relever `=D10*(1+(1-B13)*B15/B16)` and after-tax Kd `=B12*(1-B13)`), Antigravity left both broken. Gemini Native WACC 9.49% and implied share price EUR 34.19 land inside the GAF range (WACC 9.48%, price 34.20), Antigravity at WACC 9.70% and price 33.28 sits in the ‘scaffolding miss’ zone. Where Gemini Native is worse: Nothing material. Both runs share the same minor Rf sourcing (0.0351 vs GAF 0.0355) and identical structure across all 5 tabs. Overall: Gemini Native prevails. Fixing the two core WACC formula bugs is the central test of this task, Antigravity misses both, Gemini Native lands on the GAF number.”

**Why it works:** Structured “where better / where worse / overall” with concrete formulas, numerical comparisons, and a clear reason for the verdict.

#### Good Example 3: longer, dimension-by-dimension evaluation

> 1. Instruction Following. Both produce TCM documentation packages with interactive contact notes, face-to-face SOAP notes, and med reconciliation, and both cover all required TCM elements: interactive contact, face-to-face visit, medication reconciliation, CPT 99496 with correct 7-day window, and specialist referrals. Neither has content gaps.
> 2. Scope & Concision. Both are ~3x the golden's length with detailed hospitalization re-narratives that exceed the expected scope for a TCM visit. The extra narrative buries the medication decisions the visit is actually about.
> 3. Correctness. Both correctly trace sertraline, ibuprofen, aspirin, and KCl through the chart and stop both with appropriate reasoning. Antigravity discontinues CBD oil without chart support: this is a minor clinical judgment overreach but not a safety error, since it makes a call the chart left to the clinician. Gemini Native makes two errors: its insulin escalation language (‘basal-insulin bridge if FSG consistently >300’) contradicts the endocrine consult (D21), which explicitly said glipizide + diet is sufficient, and the golden's position that steroid hyperglycemia will self-correct; and it gives two conflicting carvedilol instructions (take half a 25 mg tablet AND fill a new 12.5 mg prescription), which could confuse the patient.
> 4. Professional Judgment. Antigravity is notably better than Gemini Native: both identify the 4 medication emergencies and trace each safety problem to specific source documents with chart-based reasoning, but Gemini Native's approach to hyperglycemia introduces a treatment threshold that was not part of any consultant's recommendation (the plan was written without reconciling it against the consult in the same record). The insulin escalation is the more significant error: it could lead to unnecessary treatment of a self-resolving condition.
> 5. Format & Presentation. Antigravity's medication reconciliation table is well-organized with severity ratings that help prioritize the safety issues, and its KCl and carvedilol reconciliation sections are the clearest of either attempt. Gemini Native's is similarly structured, but the contradictory carvedilol instruction undermines confidence in the reconciliation accuracy.
>
> Summary. Antigravity is notably preferred because Gemini Native introduces a clinically incorrect insulin escalation plan and gives contradictory carvedilol instructions: two errors that Antigravity avoids. Antigravity's only misstep (discontinuing CBD oil) is a minor judgment call, not a safety-relevant error.

**Why it works:** Systematically evaluates the responses across the five dimensions, both comparatively and individually. We don't require this organizational format (use whatever feels natural), but the result here is a comprehensive analysis of how well each response performs on the things that really matter.
