# Prose standard

The grammar side of the prose rules. The LLM-tell classes are in `llm-prose-tells.md`; the platform's own table is `style-guide-llm-tells.md`. These rules apply to everything we type into the platform: the rationale, the confidence note and any review note.

Correct, ordinary punctuation reads MORE human. The practitioner voice pushes toward clipped, breathless prose, which is exactly the register reviewers call "an LLM trying too hard to sound like a real, rushed, busy person". Do not loosen punctuation to sound human.

**Zero em dashes** in authored text: commas, colons, semicolons or parentheses instead; a plain hyphen is fine. In a document, density is the tell. Coded A6: any em dash in authored prose is an ERROR; a harness output docx gets a NOTE at 4 or more dashes and 12 or more per 1000 words, or at 3 or more and 8 or more per 1000.

**The three-beat rhythm, not a word list:** messy workplace detail, then artificial shorthand or idiom, then an analytical requirement, with the aphorism in the middle carrying no information. The classes, each with a fix, are in `llm-prose-tells.md`: tautologies ("Committed work is committed"), slogans ("September is use it or lose it"), idiom standing in for a quantity ("where we are naked"), compressed first-person filler ("I pulled the rest"), fragments as headings, em dashes, run-ons and comma splices, aphoristic parallelism (the semicolon-balanced maxim), scene-setting fragment openers ("Word travels."), and urgency performed instead of stated (write "This is urgent" plus the deadline; someone with a truly urgent issue does not speak in images). Coded A10 over authored prose and output documents; treat a clean A10 as the floor and read aloud. Elliptical trade jargon reads as a typo to a spelling check ("at the counter day Friday"); keep the noun, frame it grammatically.

**Comma rules:** a comma before the conjunction that joins two independent clauses (when two sentences are joined with "and" or "so" or "but", they need a comma); never a third independent clause in one sentence, whatever the conjunction; a serial comma in every list ("tires, forks, and damage"). Coded A20 (REV-REGISTER) for the two mechanical shapes: a bare conjunction opening a new subject and verb, and three or more clauses. The serial comma and the comma splice stay a read. A20 fires on nearly every first draft, which is the habit, not noise.

**Shapes that get sent back and stay a read:** comma splices ("Let me know before Friday if you want that trimmed, it is easy now"); the missing comma before a coordinating conjunction; slogan sentences and rhetorical paragraph openers ("Where the desk stands", "What comes off": open a paragraph on its subject); over-explaining the deliverable to itself ("which is on the Exceptions sheet with the arithmetic"); a sentence that points two ways at once ("takes effect on anything unshipped, which is how the pages have always read"); plural-noun-singular-verb ("our minimum on stock orders is $500" goes back; "our minimum on a stock order is $500" is the fix); run-ons chained with and/so. Desk slang in a rating note (trimmed, comes off the book, worked off, stands at) becomes plain professional English (reduced in quantity, removed from the open commitment, calculated from, remains at); figures survive the rewrite.

**The status-report skeleton (A21):** a document whose sections all run one skeleton ("remained / continue according to procedure; No X was reported") reads as generated even though each sentence is clean on its own, and A10 never sees it. A21 fires at half or more of a document's sentences on the skeleton, once the document has twenty sentences. Vary the shape section by section.

**Verbless fragments** fail a spelling and grammar read ("One workbook back, named X.xlsx" is called malformed). Write the sentence in full: "Build one workbook, named X.xlsx. It carries ...".

**Reviewer checklist mapping:** its phrase lines are coded into A10 (negative parallelism, "it's not X, it's Y", serves-as verbs, sycophancy), uniform bullet runs are A33, generic heading pairs in an output document are A32; rule of three, lexical variation and sentence-length uniformity stay a read. Its Oxford-comma line conflicts with A20's serial comma; A20 stands. The map is `llm-prose-tells.md`, class 12.

**How to apply:** plain, correctly punctuated sentences everywhere. Before submitting, sweep the rationale and the confidence note for `, (it|we|they|there|that|this|the X is)` and check each hit is a subordinate clause; audit for independent clauses chained with and/so. In review notes write "partially", never "partly" (neither is a tell; pick one form and keep it).
