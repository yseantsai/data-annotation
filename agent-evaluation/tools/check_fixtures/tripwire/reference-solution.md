Decision: APPROVE the Form I-360

According to USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 3, Part D, Chapter 2(A), a VAWA self-petitioning spouse must establish a qualifying relationship, good-faith entry into the marriage, eligibility for immigrant classification, battery or extreme cruelty, residence with the abusive spouse, and good moral character.

The evidentiary standard in VAWA cases is by “a preponderance of the evidence” (USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 3, Part D, Chapter 2(A)), meaning “more likely, than not”. In this case, USCIS decides that Marcia Evadne Whitfield has established eligibility as a VAWA self-petitioning spouse by a preponderance of the evidence.

Marcia Evadne Whitfield has established her qualifying relationship and immigrant classification: the pre-reviewed civil-status and marriage evidence establishes a valid marriage to Vernon Lloyd Lattimore, a U.S. citizen, with no unresolved prior marriage. She therefore has the qualifying spousal relationship and is eligible for immediate-relative classification. Good moral character, joint residency and good-faith-marriage: the stipulated pre-review establishes these two elements too. The coherent interview raised no issues.

Battery or extreme cruelty during the marriage is established too.

In her declaration she describes how her husband shouted at her and pointed his finger right in her face, so she was frightened (paragraph 9); how he yelled at her and called her names (paragraphs 10–11), that he demanded sex from her even when she did not want to and said no (paragraph 12); how he spat on her (paragraph14). She also describes he did not show up at her immigration appointments, and that when she complained about how he treated her, he would say that she needed him for his green card and that he could have her sent back to Jamaica whenever he wanted (paragraph16).

Her friend Desmond’s affidavit corroborates her declaration: in ¶11, page 2, he states the petitioner told him that her husband yelled at her and called her terrible names, that he took money from their account, and that he spat on her during an argument; that he refused to go to her immigration interview and later moved out. He also states that “I have known Marcia for twenty years, and I have never known her to lie. I believe her.”

Her cousin Simone in her affidavit, page 2, in the ninth narrative paragraph describes her hearing Vernon shouting at Marcia and calling her vulgar names, and yelling at her during their calls. She also states the petitioner told her about the spitting incident.

According to the USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 3, Part D, Chapter 2(E)(1), spitting is the example of a battery. The described above coercion and immigration threats support the pattern-of-control analysis for extreme cruelty, defined in the same Chapter E of the Manual.

The petitioner’s psychological report in part 9 states that “Based on my assessment over eight sessions, Ms. Whitfield’s presentation is consistent with that of an individual who has been subjected to sustained emotional and psychological abuse by an intimate partner.”

Evaluated together with the petitioner's detailed declaration, all the evidence points out the petitioner more likely than not suffered extreme cruelty and battery from her husband. The extreme cruelty or battery eligibility element is established.

There is a minor inconsistency in submitted documents, relevant to when the petitioner met her husband: in her affidavit, paragraph 4, she states “I met Vernon on Saturday, October 23, 2021, at a birthday gathering for my longtime friend Desmond Reid at Desmond’s home in East Orange.” Yet her friend Desmond in his affidavit, paragraph 5, writes “I am the one who introduced Marcia and Vernon. I invited both of them to my birthday party on October 21, 2021, at my home in East Orange, and I introduced them to each other that evening.” USCIS acknowledges this October 21/October 23 inconsistency yet decides it is understandable for two people not to recall the date of the event exactly the same, therefore, this minor conflict between dates does not undermine the petitioner’s evidence credibility.

Upon review of the totality of the record, USCIS decided that the petitioner met the burden of proof to establish her eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. The petition is APPROVED.

Why not RFE or NOID: According to the USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 3, Part D, Chapter 4 § B USCIS can issue a Request for Evidence (RFE) or Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID) where the evidence submitted does not yet establish eligibility; and its Chapter 5 § D(2) states that USCIS approves when the evidence demonstrates eligibility. The petitioner already has established eligibility as a VAWA self-petitioning spouse by a preponderance of the evidence. No request for evidence or denial is warranted.
