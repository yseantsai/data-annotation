## The Core Issue: IFRS 16 and Your Comps

This is the most important thing to get right before Thursday.

UK IFRS 16 (identical in effect to US ASC 842) requires Cragwell to capitalise its leases on the balance sheet and replace the old operating lease line in the P&L with depreciation of the right-of-use asset plus interest on the lease liability. The practical consequence is that **Cragwell's reported EBITDA is not the same thing as the EBITDA in your US comps**, and the difference is not small.

Your US-listed comps almost certainly report under US GAAP. Many US fitness operators still present an **EBITDAR** or adjusted EBITDA that adds rent back, or they present EBITDA in a way that excludes lease costs. Even where they apply ASC 842, their lease structures, owned vs. leased real estate mixes, and how their sell-side analysts strip out lease effects vary widely. Your banker needs to confirm, comp by comp, exactly what earnings figure sits beneath each of those 11.2x multiples. If even a few of them are pre-lease-cost EBITDA and Cragwell's £13.95m is post-IFRS-16 (i.e. rent is *not* in EBITDA), you are mixing apples and oranges in a way that meaningfully overstates the implied enterprise value.

**What Cragwell's EBITDA probably looks like:**

Under IFRS 16, lease payments are split into principal repayment (financing cash flow) and interest (finance charge in P&L). Neither runs through EBITDA. So Cragwell's £13.95m EBITDA is stated *after* the economic cost of occupying its sites has been partially obscured — the lease liability of £27.4m is sitting on the balance sheet instead.

You can get a rough sense of the pre-IFRS-16 "EBITDAR" equivalent by working backwards from the lease payment disclosure:

| Item | £000 |
|---|---|
| Reported EBITDA | 13,950 |
| Less: lease payments (principal + interest) | (4,840) |
| Approximate EBITDA after lease cash costs | 9,110 |

That £9.1m figure is closer to what a pre-IFRS-16 EBIT**A** would look like for lease-adjusted purposes, and it's the more honest comparison point if your US comps are also stripping leases out differently. The gap — roughly £4.8m — is not trivial at an 11.2x multiple; it represents over £50m of implied enterprise value.

## Your Net Debt Calculation Needs Revisiting

You used £7.75m of net debt (£11.6m bank loans + £3.85m cash = £7.75m net). That is the right number **only if** you are treating the lease liabilities as an operating item excluded from enterprise value — which is the EBITDAR convention.

If you are using EBITDA (post-IFRS-16, as reported), the market convention is to **include lease liabilities in net debt**, because the lease liability is the balance sheet expression of the future lease cash flows that are already inflating EBITDA relative to a pre-lease-cost measure.

Under that treatment:

| Item | £000 |
|---|---|
| Bank loans | 11,600 |
| Lease liabilities | 27,400 |
| Gross debt | 39,000 |
| Less: cash | (3,850) |
| Net debt (IFRS 16 basis) | 35,150 |

That is a very different number from £7.75m.
